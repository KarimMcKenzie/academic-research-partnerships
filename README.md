# Academic Research Partnerships

## From AI Capability to Sustained Scientific Adoption

An independent operating framework exploring how AI capabilities can translate into research partnerships, institutional adoption, and measurable scientific value.

> **Independent project:** Developed from publicly available information. This work was not commissioned by, affiliated with, or endorsed by OpenAI.

## Overview

Researcher interest is only the beginning.

Sustained AI adoption across academic research requires alignment across research workflows, technical requirements, governance, funding, procurement, researcher support, and measurable value.

This project explores how AI capabilities can be matched to meaningful funded-research workflows, tested through structured pilots, and translated into evidence-based pathways from individual researcher adoption to sustained lab and institutional use.

The strategy is organized around five questions:

| Question | Operating Focus |
|---|---|
| **Who?** | Which funded labs? |
| **What?** | Which research workflows? |
| **How?** | What must be solved? |
| **Does it work?** | What evidence matters? |
| **How does it scale?** | Who adopts next? |

## Operating Model

**IDENTIFY → DISCOVER → PILOT → ACTIVATE → RETAIN → EXPAND**

**Common operating model; research-specific implementation.**

Academic research is not a single environment. Computational research, engineering, biomedical science, behavioral science, and research involving sensitive data or additional institutional requirements can have very different workflows, infrastructure needs, funding structures, and approval pathways.

The partnership model can remain consistent while implementation adapts.

Success should ask not only whether AI makes existing work faster, but whether it enables research that was previously impractical.

## 1. Target Identification & Qualification

### Find the right funded labs, not just the biggest universities

Sponsored-research volume can show where research activity is concentrated, but institutional funding alone does not identify the laboratories most likely to become successful partners.

Public sources such as NIH RePORTER, NSF Award Search, and USAspending.gov can support a more granular opportunity funnel:

**Institution → Center / Department → Funded Lab / PI → Active Awards → Research Workflow → AI Opportunity**

> **Funding identifies where research is happening. Qualification identifies where there may be an actionable opportunity.**

### Illustrative Lab Opportunity Score

| Dimension | Weight | Qualification Question |
|---|---:|---|
| **AI Workflow Fit** | 25 | Are there meaningful workflows AI could improve or enable? |
| **Expansion Potential** | 20 | Could value move from researcher → lab → center/department → institution? |
| **Research Funding Strength** | 15 | Is this an active, durable funded-research environment? |
| **User / Champion Fit** | 15 | Are there engaged scientific sponsors and embedded users? |
| **Institutional Readiness** | 15 | Can the research environment support adoption technically, operationally, and through governance? |
| **Strategic Expansion Opportunity** | 10 | Is there a credible path to land, expand, or unblock differentiated research adoption? |

**Illustrative interpretation:**

**80–100:** Priority Pilot  
**65–79:** Further Discovery  
**50–64:** Monitor  
**<50:** Deprioritize

### Use the score to focus attention — not replace judgment

Public evidence should create a **preliminary qualification hypothesis**, not a final answer.

Discovery with researchers and institutional stakeholders should validate workflow fit, champion strength, technical and governance readiness, and the likely partnership motion before pilot selection.

The weights are illustrative starting hypotheses and should be recalibrated using actual pilot conversion, retention, and expansion evidence.

## 2. Pressure-Testing the Hypothesis

### Test the framework before scaling the framework

I pressure-tested the qualification model against a small exploratory sample of three non-obvious R1 research environments.

The objective was not statistical validation. It was to test whether public evidence could surface actionable partnership signals, expose evidence gaps, and improve the go-to-market logic before broader use.

### Missouri S&T | LAND

**Research environment:** Robotics & autonomous systems

**Public signals:** Robotics, automation and intelligent-systems signals; HPC/GPU capacity; broader AI/autonomy ecosystem; no enterprise OpenAI deployment identified in the preliminary public review.

**Discovery question:**

> Where could OpenAI establish differentiated value in existing technical research workflows?

### University of Delaware | EXPAND

**Research environment:** Interdisciplinary computational research

**Public signals:** Long-duration funded research; multidisciplinary center structure; strong research-computing resources; multi-vendor GenAI environment; an existing OpenAI foothold in one college.

**Discovery question:**

> Can an existing institutional foothold expand into advanced research workflows where OpenAI is differentiated?

### UNMC | UNBLOCK

**Research environment:** Biomedical & data-intensive research

**Public signals:** Strong biomedical/informatics infrastructure and enterprise AI procurement; broader Nebraska system ChatGPT Edu adoption; ChatGPT Edu identified as unavailable at UNMC in the preliminary public review because of stated security limitations.

**Discovery question:**

> What barrier prevents adoption today, and can an appropriate research workflow responsibly overcome it?

### What the Pressure Test Changed

| Learning | Implication |
|---|---|
| **Public Data = Signal** | Federal award data can identify funded researchers, durable portfolios, computational signals, and expansion hypotheses — but it does not establish actual user demand. |
| **Readiness Has Two Sides** | Research infrastructure matters, but technical capability does not automatically equal security, governance, procurement, or adoption readiness. |
| **Existing AI Can Help** | An existing OpenAI or competing AI footprint can indicate institutional readiness and create a path for differentiated expansion; literal “whitespace” is not always the best opportunity. |
| **Champion Fit = Discovery** | Public information can identify potential scientific leaders, but willingness to champion adoption should be validated through engagement rather than inferred. |

**Opportunity Score = Should we pursue?**

**GTM Motion = How should we pursue?**

**PUBLIC EVIDENCE → PRELIMINARY QUALIFY → LAND / EXPAND / UNBLOCK → DISCOVERY → VALIDATED OPPORTUNITY → PILOT → MEASURE / SCALE**

> Exploratory public-source findings only; current institutional conditions and stakeholder intent should be verified before outreach or pilot decisions.

## 3. Discovery & Workflow Mapping

### Understand the research before selecting the product

The goal is to move beyond:

> “This is a well-funded lab.”

And understand something more useful:

**Where could AI improve real research work, who would use it, what would the institution need to support it, and what happens next if the pilot succeeds?**

| Discovery Area | Core Question |
|---|---|
| **Research Workflow** | What is the lab trying to accomplish? Where is time or capability constrained? |
| **Users** | Who actually performs the work? Who sponsors it? |
| **Product Path** | Pro, Codex, API, ChatGPT Edu — or a combination based on the use case? |
| **Data & Technical** | What systems, data, compute, storage, and integrations are involved? |
| **Governance** | What security, privacy, contractual, sponsor, or research requirements apply? |
| **Funding** | Who pays if this works, and when is the next decision? |
| **Success** | What evidence would cause the lab or institution to continue or expand? |

### Illustrative Product Paths

| Product Path | Potential Research Context |
|---|---|
| **Pro** | Individual research and knowledge work |
| **Codex** | Research coding and software workflows |
| **API** | Custom or programmatic research workflows |
| **ChatGPT Edu** | Broader university use and institutional deployment |

> Illustrative pathways only. Actual fit depends on current product capabilities, workflow requirements, technical architecture, and institutional needs.

## The Lab Adoption Triad

Successful research adoption involves three different functions.

### Scientific Champion
**PI / Research Leader**

**Question:** Why does this matter scientifically?

### Embedded User
**RSE / Postdoc / Researcher**

**Question:** Does this improve the actual work?

### Operational Navigator
**Research Administrator / Program Manager / Research Operations**

**Question:** How do we make this work here?

The surrounding ecosystem may include:

**Research Computing • IT/Security • Sponsored Programs • Procurement • Privacy/Legal • Institutional Leadership**

> **Scientific sponsorship, daily workflow adoption, and institutional navigation are different functions; successful pilots should deliberately account for all three.**

## 4. Sustainable Adoption

### Address funding and governance early before they stall a promising pilot

**Pilot funding ≠ sustainable funding.**

At pilot inception:

- Identify the likely post-pilot funding owner.
- Generate the evidence that stakeholder will need to make the next decision.
- Align evidence generation with the relevant budget or procurement cycle.

Potential funding owners may include:

**PI/Lab • Department/Center • Research Office • CIO/Provost • Institution**

### Total Cost of Adoption

**Product Access + Compute/Storage + Technical Infrastructure + Security/Governance + Support**

A successful pilot therefore has to consider more than the price of product access.

### Governance Should Follow the Workflow

**Computational / Technical**

Research computing • APIs • code • IP • security

**Biomedical / Behavioral**

Human-subjects considerations • sensitive data • IRB/DUAs where applicable

**Higher-Governance / Clinical**

PHI/HIPAA where applicable • clinical data governance • additional institutional review

A useful operating sequence is:

**Identify workflow → requirements → approving authorities → safeguards → documentation → proceed**

### The Role of Partnerships & Programs

The partnership function should not determine university accounting or regulatory treatment on the institution's behalf.

Its role is to identify early when funding, security, privacy, procurement, or research-governance requirements could affect adoption and bring the appropriate institutional and OpenAI specialists into the conversation.

Diligence should be proportionate to the workflow and stage of adoption: low-risk experimentation should remain low-friction where appropriate, while rigor increases as data sensitivity, integration depth, and institutional scale increase.

> **The goal is not to work around institutional governance. It is to understand it early enough that a promising research use case does not get stuck later.**

## 5. Pilot & Fellows Operating Model

### Run pilots around real research work — not temporary product access

**QUALIFY → DISCOVER → DESIGN → ACTIVATE → OPERATE → MEASURE → CONVERT → SCALE**

| Timing | Phase | Primary Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-pilot** | Qualify + Discover | Pilot Readiness Brief |
| **Days 1–15** | Design | Pilot Charter |
| **Days 16–30** | Activate | Meaningful workflow use |
| **Days 31–60** | Operate | Usage + learning |
| **Days 61–75** | Measure | Pilot Scorecard |
| **Days 76–90** | Convert | Adoption decision |

### Illustrative Pilot

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| **User** | Computational biology Fellow |
| **Problem** | Repetitive pipeline / debugging work |
| **Path** | Codex pilot |
| **Evidence** | Workflow use + completion time + retained use |
| **Next** | Expand to additional RSEs if evidence supports it |

A pilot should make **the researcher, the work, the evidence, and the next decision visible.**

> **Access ≠ Activation. Workflow use = Activation.**

### Fellows as Embedded Adopters

Fellows can include graduate researchers, postdocs, research software engineers, analysts, or other embedded research personnel selected for:

- Workflow fit
- Engagement potential
- Scientific sponsorship
- Ability to generate reusable learning

The strongest Fellow is positioned to test a **meaningful workflow** — not simply someone interested in AI.

### Measure Value, Then Make an Explicit Decision

| Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **STOP** | Insufficient value or unacceptable barriers |
| **MODIFY** | Promising; adjust workflow or implementation |
| **CONTINUE** | Evidence supports retained use |
| **EXPAND** | Evidence supports broader adoption |

Common scorecard dimensions include:

**Activation • Retained Usage • Workflow Value • Research Value • User Experience • Technical Fit • Institutional Fit • Expansion Signal**

> **Time evidence generation to the decision cycle of the stakeholder most likely to fund the next stage.**

## 6. Scale, Learn & Contribute

### Use each successful pilot to improve the next one

**IDENTIFY → PILOT → EVIDENCE → RETAIN → EXPAND → COMMUNITY → LEARN**

Each cohort should leave behind evidence, lessons, and resources that make the next one stronger.

Researcher community can be built around each cohort through shared onboarding, recurring office hours, cross-lab exchange, and reusable workflow resources — turning isolated pilots into a network of researchers learning from one another.

### How Adoption Can Grow

**Researcher → Lab → Center / Department → Institution**

Expansion should follow:

- Demonstrated value
- Repeatability
- Institutional feasibility
- Sustainable funding
- Demand

### What OpenAI Learns

**Researcher Evidence → Partnerships & Programs → Solutions / Product / Education → Better Next Deployment**

Each pilot can generate reusable evidence about:

- Research workflows
- Product friction
- Institutional barriers
- Emerging use cases
- Onboarding needs
- Case studies
- Adoption patterns

The objective is not simply to scale individual deployments.

It is to make **the next deployment better informed than the last one.**

## Questions Worth Studying Across Cohorts

Academic partnerships can also function as a research observatory.

Questions worth studying include:

- How does researcher trust in AI develop?
- How should AI assistance be attributed?
- Does value come primarily from faster work, or from newly possible work?
- Who ultimately funds sustained adoption?
- Which institutional barriers repeatedly slow otherwise promising use cases?
- How does AI change what researchers can attempt?

These questions can turn individual partnership activity into broader organizational learning.

## Methodology

This is an independent operating analysis developed from publicly available information about academic research funding, research institutions, AI adoption, and OpenAI products and programs.

The project uses public evidence to form **preliminary opportunity hypotheses**, not conclusions about researcher demand, institutional intent, product fit, security approval, procurement readiness, or partnership interest.

Public evidence is therefore treated as the beginning of discovery rather than a substitute for it.

The qualification weights, partnership motions, operating frameworks, pilot structure, and scaling model presented here are independent analysis and illustrative hypotheses intended to be tested and refined through evidence.
